Gaushalas (Cow Shelters)

Gaushalas are an integral part of Indian culture, where the cow is revered as a mother. In cities, keeping cows is difficult, and gaushalas give them a safe home.

Why gaushalas matter

  • Preserve our cultural heritage
  • Dung and urine used for organic farming and medicine
  • Fewer road accidents caused by stray cows
  • Cleaner streets
  • Social harmony
  • Support for rituals and worship

Our three gaushalas

Kadwapani

Karbari Grant, Shimla Bypass Road, Dehradun

Chidibhelli

Timli, Vikasnagar, Dehradun, Uttarakhand

Khodri Majri

Near Dakpather Barrage, Himachal Pradesh

Together the gaushalas can accommodate around 2,000 cows and 50 staff.

How the gaushalas are managed

  1. Infrastructure

    Sheds for the cows and quarters for staff.

  2. Fodder

    Fresh green fodder from local farms, cut and mixed with dry husk, with wheat, pulses, jaggery and salt as supplements.

  3. Basic facilities

    Reliable electricity and clean drinking water.

  4. Dedicated staff

    Staff for feeding, fodder cutting, dung cleaning and overall cleanliness.

  5. Medical services

    On-site care by a veterinary team for sick and injured cows, including accident cases.

  6. Respectful last rites

    Deceased cows are laid to rest with respect and hygiene.
